Water Heater Repair in Round Rock, TX

Water heater diagnosis and repair for cold water, leaks, and failed controls.

Call (737) 215-4933 Ask a question

A shower that runs cold and a water heater that puts water on the utility-room floor both get described as a broken water heater, but they send us toward different components. From the hallway, a dry closet and cold taps point toward a burner, heating element, thermostat, ignition control, or gas supply; a puddle, damp pan, or water track at the tank points toward a valve, fitting, or tank seam. We diagnose and repair both tank and tankless water heaters.

We check the floor, pan, piping, and temperature source before choosing a repair, because a failed thermostat does not call for the same work as a leaking tank. A dry heater that has stopped heating can usually wait for a planned repair if no gas odor, burning smell, or electrical damage is present. Water escaping from the tank, a hot-water line, or the relief valve should not wait because it can damage framing, drywall, and the burner or electrical compartment.

Cold showers and puddles

We hear no hot water, hot water that runs out after a few minutes, a pilot that keeps going out, and water pooling under the heater. A popping or rumbling sound from the tank can point to sediment around the burner or lower element, while a clicking ignition sequence without heat can point to a control, igniter, or fuel-supply problem. Each symptom gives us a different starting point at the tank or service panel.

A relief valve that drips into a discharge pipe does not mean the same thing as water seeping from a fitting above the tank. Rust-colored water at one hot tap can also come from a different source than a wet tank jacket or corroded connection. These faults can sound alike over the phone, but the part involved, the labor needed, and the repair cost can differ once we inspect the heater.

Tank and burner callout

At a tank and burner callout, we begin with the model plate, fuel type, visible piping, shutoff valves, and the area around the tank. We measure water temperature, check incoming pressure, and inspect the temperature-and-pressure relief valve, whose discharge pipe should terminate near the floor. For many homes, we compare the delivered water temperature with a setting around 120 degrees Fahrenheit and check whether supply pressure approaches the 80 psi range where pressure control may be needed.

Basic isolation and measurements often take 30 to 60 minutes, while a heater with concealed wiring, venting faults, or water damage can take longer to trace. We can commonly replace a failed thermostat, heating element, thermocouple, igniter, relief valve, or accessible fitting during the same visit when the correct part is available. A leaking tank body, a corroded vent connection, a replacement heater, or work requiring a permit inspection is normally planned as separate work after we identify the scope.

Shutoffs, access, and parts

The heater’s age, the condition of the shutoff valve, and access to the tank all move the repair scope. An accessible thermostat behind a service panel calls for less disassembly than a heater boxed into a closet with a tight door opening, finished wall, or stored items around the piping. A newer unit with a readable model plate also makes it easier for us to match an element, gas valve, control board, or anode rod.

A seized cold-water shutoff can add work because we may need to isolate the line before removing a valve or fitting. A discontinued control, uncommon gas valve, or tankless component may need to be ordered rather than fitted during the first visit. We also account for whether a permit or inspection sign-off applies to a replacement heater, vent alteration, or gas connection before the work begins.

Model plate and closet access

Before calling, find the manufacturer’s model and serial number on the data plate, usually attached to the tank jacket or tankless cabinet. Tell us whether the heater serves a bathroom, kitchen, or the whole house, and whether hot water stops at every fixture or only one tap. Note whether the pilot is out, the display shows a code, the relief pipe is dripping, or water is running across the floor.

Please let us know the approximate age of the heater and whether a closet door, attic opening, or garage path limits access to the tank. Clear a safe path to the heater if water, stored boxes, or shelving blocks the shutoff and service panel.
