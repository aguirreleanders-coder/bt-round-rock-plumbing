Drain Cleaning in Round Rock, TX

We clear clogged sinks, tubs, showers, and floor drains across Round Rock.

Call (737) 215-4933 Ask a question

A kitchen sink that gurgles after the faucet shuts off, a shower that holds ankle-deep water, or a sour odor at a floor drain often points to material restricting the pipe. We trace the fixture, trap, branch drain, and accessible cleanout instead of treating every slow drain as the same clog. We handle drain cleaning for homes in Round Rock, Brushy Creek, Windemere, and Wells Branch.

Grease, soap film, hair, wipes, and sediment collect in different sections of a drain line and can leave water moving slowly long before it stops completely. A basin that only drains slowly can sometimes wait while you limit use of that fixture, but standing wastewater, repeated backups, or water rising in a tub should not wait because a growing blockage can overflow through a lower drain or damage the surrounding floor and cabinet.

Gurgling drains and sour odors

People often call us about a sink that burps air, a shower that drains only after the water is turned off, or a floor drain with a sewer-like smell. A kitchen sink may fill while a dishwasher runs, especially when grease has narrowed the branch pipe beyond the trap. A toilet flush that makes water lift in the nearby tub can also point to a restriction farther along the shared drain route.

Water coming back into a shower, a laundry standpipe that spills, and a single bathroom sink that drains slowly do not lead us to the same starting point. A dry trap can cause an odor without a clog, while a partial blockage can cause gurgling without visible standing water. Those symptoms can look similar from the room, but the pipe location, access point, and cleaning method can differ, so the work involved can differ as well.

Drain inspection and cable work

At the callout, we ask which fixture backs up first, then run water at the sink, tub, shower, or laundry connection to see how the drain responds. We check the trap, visible pipe joints, cleanout cap, and nearby fixtures for a shared backup. A basic fixture check can take roughly 15 to 30 minutes, while a blockage farther down a branch or main drain can require more time to locate and clear.

We identify the accessible pipe size before selecting a cable or cutting tool; many sink drains use 1 1/2-inch pipe, while tubs, toilets, and larger drain routes commonly use wider pipe. We clear reachable buildup on the visit when the cleanout and pipe route allow it, then run water again to confirm flow through the fixture. If a camera inspection, concealed-pipe access, or repair beyond the blockage is needed, we explain that next step and arrange separate work rather than opening a wall or digging without locating the route.

Cleanout access and pipe material

A cleanout in a garage, crawlspace, exterior wall, or cabinet gives us a more direct route to the blockage than a drain opening beneath a strainer. A cleanout cap that has seized, a trap buried behind a vanity, or a pipe route hidden under a slab can add time before cable work even begins. Access through a roof vent or a tight crawlspace also changes the equipment and setup the drain requires.

Pipe material and condition matter because aging cast iron, thin galvanized pipe, ABS, PVC, and older fittings do not all respond the same way to a cable or cutting head. A simple buildup near a sink trap calls for different work than roots, scale, or a collapsed section farther down the line. If inspection points to a damaged buried pipe, required sign-off, or a component that must be ordered, we separate that work from the drain clearing so the scope stays clear.

Fixture room and cleanout access

Before calling, note which room has the slow or backed-up fixture and whether the sink, shower, tub, laundry drain, or floor drain affects another fixture nearby. Let us know whether water is standing now, whether the problem follows laundry or dishwasher use, and whether any drain has overflowed onto the floor. If you can see a cleanout cap, tell us whether it sits in a cabinet, garage, yard, crawlspace, or exterior wall.

Clear items away from the sink cabinet, shower drain, or cleanout area if you can do so safely, but do not force a cap or pour more chemical cleaner into a blocked pipe. Mention any drain cleaner already used so we can handle the trap and drain opening appropriately.
