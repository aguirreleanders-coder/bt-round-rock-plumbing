Drain Cleaning in Round Rock, TX

We clear slow sinks, backed-up tubs, and blocked drains across Round Rock.

Call (737) 215-4933 Ask a question

A sink that gurgles after the faucet shuts off, a tub that holds water around your feet, or a floor drain with a sewer smell usually points to buildup somewhere past the fixture opening. Water may drain slowly for days before grease, soap residue, hair, or food scraps close off enough of the pipe to stop it completely. A kitchen sink can also back up into the other bowl when the shared branch line cannot carry water away.

We check the fixture drain, trap, branch pipe, and accessible cleanout to locate the blockage rather than treating every slow drain as the same problem. We cover drain cleaning calls in Round Rock, Brushy Creek, Windemere, and Wells Branch, including sinks, tubs, showers, and floor drains. A drain that only slows can be scheduled before it stops, but water rising in a sink, tub, or floor drain should not wait because the blockage can overflow into cabinets, flooring, or the next connected fixture.

Gurgling sinks and standing water

Customers often call about a kitchen sink that fills while the dishwasher drains, a bathroom basin that empties slowly, or a shower pan that leaves a ring of water after use. A tub may bubble at the drain while the nearby toilet flushes, which can mean water is meeting resistance in a shared branch pipe. Some callers notice a rotten sewer smell from a floor drain, while others find black water coming up through a shower drain or laundry standpipe.

A sink stopper packed with hair, a trap holding grease, and a blockage farther down a drain line can all make the same fixture drain slowly. Gurgling can also come from a partial obstruction that pulls air through the trap as water moves past it. These symptoms sound similar over the phone, but the fixture, pipe route, and location of the buildup call for different labor and equipment, which is why we inspect before defining the work.

Drain inspection and cable run

We begin the callout by asking which fixture backs up first, running water at the affected drain, and checking whether another sink, tub, or floor drain reacts. We remove the stopper or trap when access allows, inspect the visible pipe for buildup, and look for an accessible cleanout on the branch or main line. Kitchen and bathroom drain pipes commonly measure from 1 1/2 to 2 inches across, so we match the cable and cutting head to the opening and pipe size. We also note the route from the fixture to the cleanout, especially where a cabinet, crawlspace, or wall limits access.

A stopper cleaning or trap check may take 15 to 30 minutes, while a cable run through an accessible branch drain commonly takes 30 to 90 minutes depending on the pipe route and material in the line. During the same visit, we clear reachable buildup, flush the fixture repeatedly, and check that the trap and drain connection reseal correctly. We refit the trap and replace a worn slip-joint washer only if it will not seal after reassembly. A blockage beyond the accessible cleanout, a damaged pipe, or a line that needs further investigation may require a return visit after we establish where the obstruction sits.

Cleanouts, pipe route, and buildup

A capped cleanout near the fixture, outside wall, or yard gives us a direct route into the drain pipe and usually reduces the amount of cabinet or fixture access needed. A cleanout hidden behind shelving, a tight vanity, or finished wall material adds setup and removal work before a cable can enter the line. The route also matters: a short bathroom branch differs from a drain pipe that travels under a slab or across a long section of the house.

Hair and soap residue near a shower trap often clear differently from hardened grease in a kitchen branch or roots entering an exterior line. A removable trap under a sink gives us one access point, while a toilet, floor drain, or concealed pipe may require a different entry route. If cleaning reveals a broken section, recurring root entry, or work that moves beyond the drain opening, the pipe condition and any required approval can change the scope from a straightforward clearing to follow-up work.

Fixture and cleanout location

Before calling, note which room and fixture has the problem, whether it is the kitchen sink, shower, tub, bathroom basin, or floor drain. Tell us whether water is standing now, whether another fixture makes it bubble, and whether the backup appeared after using the dishwasher, washing machine, or garbage disposal. If you can safely see a capped cleanout pipe in a cabinet, garage, yard, or crawlspace, let us know where it sits.

A photo of the sink cabinet, trap, disposal, or floor drain can show the access around the pipe before we arrive. Mention any drain cleaner already poured into the fixture so we can handle the trap and standing water safely.
