Leak Repair in Round Rock, TX

We trace and repair dripping pipes, wet cabinets, and concealed water leaks.

Call (737) 215-4933 Ask a question

A slow drip beneath a bathroom sink can leave only a damp cabinet floor at first. After a week, the particleboard can swell around the pipe opening, the cabinet backing can soften, and water can reach baseboards or flooring beyond the vanity. A pinhole in a copper line or a loose compression nut can follow the same pattern, with moisture appearing farther from the actual fitting.

Over a month or a season, repeated moisture can stain drywall, loosen tile grout, corrode a shutoff valve, and feed mold growth inside a wall cavity. We trace the water to the pipe, valve, joint, trap, or supply connection that released it rather than treating the visible stain as the source. Leak repair should not wait when water is reaching wood, drywall, insulation, or a concealed floor area, because each additional run of water enlarges the material that needs repair.

Water under the sink

Customers often call about water under the sink, a pipe that drips only while the faucet runs, or a wet ring around the base of a toilet. A steady hiss inside a wall, a ceiling spot below an upstairs bathroom, or a cabinet that smells musty can point to water escaping where it should not. Some leaks show up as a puddle after using a fixture, while others leave a dry-looking pipe and a wet wall several feet away.

A water meter that continues moving after fixtures are shut off can indicate a supply-line leak, while a drain leak may appear only during a shower, laundry load, or sink use. Warm flooring, peeling paint, and a wet patch near a refrigerator line can also come from different fittings and routes. A loose packing nut, cracked trap, failed wax seal, split supply tube, and pipe joint can look similar from the room, but each fault needs its own repair work and carries a different material and access cost.

Pressure test and inspection

We begin the callout by locating the wet area, asking when the water appears, and checking the nearby fixture, supply valves, drain connections, and exposed pipe. We can isolate a sink, toilet, washing-machine box, or exterior hose bib to narrow down the route before opening a wall or ceiling. A meter isolation check can take 10 to 15 minutes, while a concealed leak may need additional testing through the affected room and adjoining spaces.

For water-supply piping, we check pressure with a gauge and look for readings outside the common 40 to 80 psi range that can strain fittings and valve seals. We inspect copper, PEX, PVC, threaded connections, compression joints, braided supply lines, and shutoff valves for corrosion, cracks, and movement. An exposed supply tube, trap washer, angle stop, or accessible joint can often be replaced during the same visit; pipe behind tile, within a slab, or behind a finished wall may require a planned return after access, drying, or ordered parts are arranged.

After replacing a fitting or section of pipe, we restore water slowly and watch the joint under normal pressure for 15 to 30 minutes. We also run the connected fixture when the leak involves a drain or trap, because a drain joint can stay dry until water moves through it. That final check confirms whether the repair addressed the route that created the stain or puddle.

Shutoff valves and wall access

An exposed braided supply tube beneath a sink generally takes less access work than a split pipe behind a tiled shower wall or inside a ceiling cavity. The age and material of the fitting matter because old galvanized connections, corroded copper joints, and brittle plastic valves can break apart during removal. A newer compression stop with clear cabinet access usually involves fewer steps than a valve buried behind stored items, trim, or drywall.

A seized shutoff valve can change the repair because we may need to shut down water at another point before removing the leaking part. Specialty cartridges, discontinued fixture parts, matching trim, or a valve that must be ordered can move work to a later visit. If a repair requires opening a wall, rerouting pipe, or work subject to local inspection requirements, the access route and any required sign-off also affect the scope.

Fixture model and leak location

Before calling, note which room has the leak and which fixture was running when the water appeared. If the leak involves a dishwasher, washing machine, refrigerator line, or built-in fixture, find the model tag, often printed on a door edge, side panel, or manufacturer plate. Tell us whether water is running now, whether the nearby shutoff valve closes, and whether the wet area sits under a cabinet, behind a wall, or below a ceiling.

A photo of the pipe, valve, supply tube, or stain can help identify the connection and access space before the visit. Call BT Round Rock Plumbing at (737) 215-4933 before continued water soaks the cabinet, drywall, flooring, or ceiling around the leak.
