Sewer Line Repair in Round Rock, TX

Camera inspection and repair planning for damaged sewer lines in Round Rock.

Call (737) 215-4933 Ask a question

Older houses in Round Rock and established properties in Brushy Creek, Windemere, and Wells Branch may still have cast-iron, clay, or Orangeburg sewer pipe below the yard. Pipe joints can open as soil moves, roots can enter through seams, and aging pipe walls can soften, crack, or collapse below a driveway or slab. We trace the line from the cleanout before recommending a repair route.

A sewer line can also fail in a newer property where construction debris, root growth, or a poorly supported connection restricts the pipe. Wastewater may stop at a low point, collect behind a break, or push back toward the lowest drain in the house. We check the direction of the pipe, the condition of the interior wall, and the location of the blockage or damaged section.

A suspected sewer line fault should not wait when wastewater backs up into a shower, toilet, or floor drain, or when sewage reaches the yard. A damaged building sewer can release wastewater into soil and allow a small separation to become a larger collapse or washout.

Backed-up drains and sewer odor

A toilet that bubbles when the washing machine drains, a shower drain that fills from below, or several fixtures that stop draining at once can point to the building sewer. Customers also report gurgling from a tub, water appearing at a floor drain, or sewage coming up through the lowest bathroom fixture. A sewer odor around the cleanout, a wet patch of lawn, or a soft area near the pipe route can signal a break outside the house.

Roots inside a pipe, a separated joint, a belly holding wastewater, and a crushed section can produce similar sounds and backups. A single blockage near a fixture can also look like a sewer problem until we inspect the line. Each fault calls for different materials, access work, and repair scope, so we use the camera view and locator reading to separate them before planning the work.

Camera inspection and locating

We start at the accessible cleanout and run a sewer camera through the pipe to view roots, standing water, offsets, cracks, and collapsed sections. The camera head includes a transmitter that lets us mark the route from above ground and estimate the depth of the damaged area. A camera run and surface locate commonly take about 45 to 90 minutes, depending on the length of the pipe, the condition of the cleanout, and whether debris stops the camera.

We note pipe material, joint condition, the direction of flow, and the point where the camera cannot continue. Many residential building sewers use 4-inch pipe, although older homes can have different materials and transitions at the house connection. We can usually complete the inspection, locating, and repair discussion during the visit; excavation, pipe replacement, permits, or work below concrete may need a separately scheduled repair date.

Pipe material and excavation route

Pipe material changes the repair approach. Clay pipe often has separated bell joints and root entry, cast iron can rust from the inside, and Orangeburg pipe can deform or flatten with age. A short damaged section near an exposed cleanout takes a different repair plan than a long run beneath a driveway, mature tree, retaining wall, or slab.

Access also affects the work needed to reach the pipe. A shallow line in open soil may allow a focused excavation, while a deep route, tight side yard, or pipe crossing under concrete requires more site preparation and restoration. Permit requirements, utility locating, a seized cleanout cap, and replacement fittings that must be ordered can also change the scope before we open the ground.

Cleanout location and pipe age

Before calling, look for the sewer cleanout and note whether it sits near the house, beside a driveway, in a flower bed, or behind a gate. Tell us which fixture backs up first, whether the toilet, shower, or floor drain has wastewater in it now, and whether sewage has reached the lawn. If you know the home’s approximate construction date or whether the sewer pipe has been repaired before, that helps us anticipate clay, cast iron, plastic, or Orangeburg pipe.

Keep vehicles, planters, and stored items away from the cleanout or suspected pipe route if access permits. Photos of the wet area, cleanout, or affected drain can help us understand the layout before arrival.
