Sewer Line Repair in Round Rock, TX

Camera inspections and sewer line repairs for Round Rock-area properties.

Call (737) 215-4933 Ask a question

Older homes and properties with aging cast-iron, clay, or early plastic pipe can develop sewer trouble below the yard, slab, or driveway. Around Round Rock, Brushy Creek, Windemere, and Wells Branch, tree roots, shifting soil, separated joints, and worn pipe walls can stop wastewater from reaching the municipal connection. We trace the line from the cleanout toward the blockage or damaged section before recommending a repair.

A sewer line problem should not wait once wastewater backs up, a toilet bubbles during a shower, or sewage reaches a floor drain. Continued use sends more water through a damaged pipe, which can spread sewage into a bathroom, laundry room, or yard and make the affected section harder to reach safely. We inspect the pipe route and help determine what needs repair before the backup causes further damage.

Sewage odor and gurgling drains

Customers often call after hearing gurgling from a toilet while the washing machine drains, seeing water rise in a tub, or finding a floor drain that will not clear. A sewage odor near a cleanout, bathroom, or side yard can also point to a cracked sewer pipe, an open joint, or a blocked line. Slow drainage throughout several fixtures differs from one sink that drains slowly because the restriction may sit farther down the main line.

Water may appear at the cleanout cap, around a toilet base, or in the lowest shower or tub first because those openings sit closest to the sewer line. Roots inside a clay joint, a collapsed pipe wall, a low section holding solids, and a blockage at the connection can produce similar sounds and backups. Each fault calls for different materials, access work, and repair scope, so the cause needs a camera view or line test before anyone can sort out the likely cost.

Camera inspection and line locating

We begin the visit at the accessible cleanout and check which fixtures trigger the backup, where water appears, and whether the cap, riser, or drain opening provides a usable entry point. A camera inspection commonly takes about 30 to 60 minutes, depending on the pipe route, standing water, and amount of debris in the line. We watch for root intrusion, offset joints, cracks, bellies, and changes between cast iron, clay, ABS, and PVC pipe.

We measure the line route from the cleanout, identify the approximate damaged area, and note whether the pipe is commonly 3 or 4 inches in diameter. On a suitable first visit, we can inspect the line, locate the trouble area, and explain the repair path. Excavation, replacement of a buried section, utility marking, and any required permit or inspection are normally scheduled after the pipe route and access conditions have been confirmed.

Pipe depth and yard access

The material and condition of the buried pipe affect the repair scope. A cracked clay section, corroded cast-iron pipe, separated coupling, or crushed plastic line may require a different replacement method and different fittings. A line that has failed at several joints can also change the decision from replacing one section to addressing a longer run.

Access around the pipe route matters just as much as the break itself. A cleanout beside an open side yard gives a different excavation path than a line below a driveway, patio, landscaping bed, slab edge, or fence. Utility locations, a seized cleanout cap, a section that needs special-order pipe or couplings, and city sign-off requirements can add steps before the repair can be completed.

Cleanout location and pipe material

Before calling, check where the cleanout sits and whether it is in the front yard, side yard, garage, bathroom, or near the foundation. Note which fixtures back up first, whether sewage or water is running out now, and whether the problem occurs when a toilet flushes, a shower drains, or the washing machine discharges. If an earlier repair left a cap, receipt, camera report, or pipe material note, keep it available for the visit.

A clear photo of the cleanout and the surrounding fence, driveway, shrubs, or concrete can help us plan access to the pipe route. Let us know if a gate stays locked, a vehicle blocks the cleanout, or a pet uses the yard where excavation may be needed.
